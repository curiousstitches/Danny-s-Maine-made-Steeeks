Pet-Friendly Steeecks

Chewable and display-only pieces, made with the right wood and finish for the job.

For Every Animal

Not just for dogs

If your pet likes to carry, chew, climb, or just be near a steeeck — dog, cat, rabbit, reptile, or something else entirely — we can probably make something for them. Every pet piece falls into one of two categories, and we always tell you which one you're getting.

Made to Chew

Chewable Pet Steeecks

Built for pets that carry, gnaw, or chew — made only from wood and finishes that are safe if your pet actually eats a little bit of it while they play. These wear down faster and need to be replaced periodically.

Made to Show

Display-Only Pet Steeecks

Small decorative pieces meant to sit on a pet's back or nearby as they walk around — never in the mouth. Because these aren't chewed, we can use a wider range of woods and a fully protective, long-lasting sealed finish.

The Basics

What actually makes a piece "pet-friendly"

It's not one thing — it's every step of how a piece is chosen, made, and finished.

The wood itself is verified safe

Only species with a well-documented history of being safe if chewed or licked go into a pet piece. If there's real debate about a wood's safety, we don't use it — no exceptions.

It's untreated and chemical-free

No pesticides, no pressure-treated lumber, no scrap wood of unknown origin. Every piece is sourced, cleaned, and fully dried before it's shaped.

The finish matches the use

Food-grade oil or wax for anything that might be chewed; standard sealants only for pieces that never touch a mouth. Never the wrong finish on the wrong piece.

It's shaped and sanded for safety

Smooth, splinter-free, sized appropriately for the animal — no small parts that could break off and become a choking hazard.

Chewable Pieces

Woods we use for chewable steeecks

Every chewable piece is made from a wood that's widely considered safe if gnawed or splintered off in small amounts. Harder woods last longer before they need replacing; softer ones wear down ("wilt") faster but are gentler on puppy teeth.

Longest Lasting

Coffee wood

Extremely hard and dense. Tends to flake into small fibers rather than sharp splinters as it wears, which is considered a lower-risk way for a chew piece to break down.

Durable

Maple

A dense hardwood that holds up well to steady chewing and has minimal natural odor. One of the most common woods used in pet chew products.

Durable

Birch

Hard and relatively splinter-resistant compared to softer woods, with a similar wear pattern to maple.

Wears Faster

Olive wood

Dense but slightly softer than maple or birch. Naturally contains beneficial oils. Good for pets who chew more gently.

We do not use pine, cedar, cherry, black walnut, oak, or any wood with a history of toxicity concerns for chewable pieces — even if it looks great, if there's any real debate about whether it's safe to chew, it doesn't go in a chew piece. Wood for chew pieces is always sourced untreated, cleaned, fully dried, and sanded smooth before it ever reaches your pet.

Because of the extra care this takes, chewable pieces generally take a bit longer to make than our standard walking steeecks — we'd rather take the additional time than rush a piece your pet is going to gnaw on.

Coatings

Finishes on chewable vs. display-only pieces

This is the biggest safety difference between the two product types — and why we never use a display finish on something meant to be chewed.

What's on chewable pieces?

Only food-grade, fully-cured finishes that are safe even if licked or lightly chewed: pure food-grade mineral oil, raw or organic beeswax, or 100% pure tung oil (fully cured, not a solvent-blended "tung oil finish"). These soak into the wood instead of forming a hard film, so there's nothing to flake or chip off in a piece that could be swallowed.

What's on display-only pieces?

Since these never go in a pet's mouth, we can use the same protective, long-lasting finishes as our regular steeecks — including sealed polyurethane — for a piece that holds up to weather and years of wear without needing constant reapplication.

Why not use the tough finish on chew pieces too?

Standard wood finishes (polyurethane, lacquer, most varnishes) aren't meant to be eaten. Small chipped-off flakes could be swallowed, and some finishes release fumes that are more irritating to a pet's smaller lungs during curing. It's simply not worth the risk on something designed to be gnawed on.

Do chewable finishes need to be reapplied?

Yes — food-grade oil and wax finishes wear off with regular chewing and handling. That's expected and normal; it's the tradeoff for a finish that's fully safe to ingest.

For Cats

Catnip-infused steeecks

Cats don't chew wood the way dogs do, but a small wood chew steeeck rubbed or infused with catnip (or silver vine, a similar herb some cats respond to even more strongly) is a real, well-established product — it's the same idea behind store-bought "matatabi" sticks.

How We Do It

Surface-rubbed catnip

Dried catnip is rubbed directly into the rough, untreated grain of the wood, which naturally absorbs the scent and oils. Simple, safe, and easy to "recharge" at home by rubbing in more dried catnip when the scent fades.

How We Do It

Refillable cavity

For a longer-lasting option, we can drill a small cavity into the piece and pack it with loose dried catnip behind a breathable section of wood — letting scent seep out over time, refillable when it fades.

Catnip itself is a natural, non-toxic herb that's safe even if a cat licks, chews, or eats a little of it. Since cats do sometimes nibble these, we use the same safe, untreated wood and food-grade finish rules as our dog-chewable pieces. Want silver vine instead of catnip, or a mix of both? Just ask in your order notes.

Beyond Dogs & Cats

Rabbits, reptiles, birds & more

"Pet" doesn't just mean dog to us. Tell us about your animal and what they like to do with a steeeck, and we'll figure out the right piece.

Gnawing Pets

Rabbits & small rodents

Animals that gnaw constantly (rabbits, guinea pigs, chinchillas) need genuinely untreated, safe wood — we treat these the same as our chewable dog pieces, using the same safe-wood list above.

Climbing Pets

Reptiles & climbers

For basking or climbing branches, the priority is a stable, non-toxic-if-licked surface rather than chew-durability. We'll build to size and finish based on your enclosure and species — just ask.

Perching Pets

Birds

Perches use different woods than chew pieces — manzanita, apple, maple, and birch are all considered safe and are commonly used for bird perches. We avoid cedar and avocado wood entirely for anything bird-related, since both carry real toxicity concerns for birds specifically.

Curious Pets

Ferrets & other gnawers

If your pet likes to carry or mouth objects but isn't listed here, tell us about them — we'll pick from our safe-wood list and build to a size that makes sense for them.

Not sure what your pet needs? Tell us about them in the custom order notes and we'll figure it out together.

Important

Safety notes

Always supervise chewing

Any wood chew, no matter how carefully made, should be used under supervision. Remove and replace any piece once it's worn down to a small size or has any sharp broken edges.

Every animal is different

Sensitivities vary by individual animal, breed, and species. If your pet has known allergies, a sensitive stomach, or a medical condition, check with your vet before introducing a new wood chew.

Stop if anything seems off

If your pet shows vomiting, drooling, mouth irritation, or unusual behavior after chewing, stop use and contact your veterinarian right away.
